## 3. Hooks, titler og thumbnails

### Hook-mønstre (de første 5–30 sekunder)

1. **Seeren sættes ind i scenen:** "Imagine you …" / "You wake up and …". Seeren er hovedpersonen fra første sætning.
2. **Forventningsbrud.** Et dokumenteret BrightSide-eksempel: *"Plastic surgery often gets a bad reputation, but what happens when it goes exactly as planned?"* Seeren forventer "gone wrong" og får det modsatte ([Databox](https://databox.com/how-to-write-youtube-video-title)).
3. **Indsatsen gøres tydelig:** liv eller død, et tidspres eller en rekord.
4. **En chokerende, konkret fakta** i første sætning.
5. **Et løfte om en rejse:** "By day 7 …" eller "all the way to the center". Strukturen er i sig selv et åbent spørgsmål, som seeren vil have svar på.
6. **En myte, der bliver skudt ned:** "Everyone thinks X. They're wrong."

### Greb, der holder seeren gennem videoen

- **Progression:** Dag, dybde eller trin tælles op på skærmen.
- **Åbne spørgsmål:** "But the strangest part comes at day 5 …"
- **Et quiz-spørgsmål midt i videoen:** "Write your guess in the comments", med svaret senere. Det giver kommentarer, og seeren bliver hængende.
- **Billedet skifter hvert 3.–8. sekund**, med lydeffekter og en figur, der reagerer.
- **Kapitler/tidsstempler** i beskrivelsen.
- **Afslutningen** er et spørgsmål til kommentarfeltet og en slutskærm, der sender seeren videre til næste video.

### Titelformler

| Formel | Eksempel |
|---|---|
| What If You [ekstrem handling] for [tid]? | What If You Stopped Sleeping for a Week? |
| What Happens to Your Body If/At [situation]? | What Happens to Your Body at the Mariana Trench Bottom? |
| The Only Way to Survive [fare] | The Only Way to Survive a Free Fall from Plane |
| [N] Tips on How to Survive [trussel] | 13 Tips on How to Survive Wild Animal Attacks |
| Never [hverdagshandling]: [N] Reasons Why | Never Sleep on your Side or Stomach: 5 Reasons Why |
| [N] Reasons Why You'll Always Be [negativ] | 15 Reasons Why You'll Always be Poor |
| [N] Signs You're [relaterbar situation] | – |
| Why [almindelig ting] Is Actually [uventet] | – |

### Thumbnails

Før/efter-billeder side om side, røde pile og cirkler, chokerede ansigter, én tydelig scene og kraftige kontraster ([Databox](https://databox.com/how-to-write-youtube-video-title)). Thumbnailen vækker følelsen, titlen giver den rationelle grund til at klikke, og hooket indfrier løftet ([Tubics](https://www.tubics.com/blog/The-Tension-Triangle)).

### Stemme og billeder

En afslappet, underholdende fortællerstemme lagt over tegnet 2D-animation med simple figurer. Ældre videoer bruger også stockfootage. Tempoet er højt, med mange lydeffekter.

## 4. Svagheder, du kan udnytte

- **Mange faktuelle fejl.** Wikipedia har konsensus om, at kanalen er en upålidelig kilde, og RationalWiki kalder dele af indholdet pseudovidenskab. Kritikere nævner blandt andet påstande om, at citron i øjnene er godt for dem, vildledende videoer om Titanic og udokumenterede påstande om søvn ([Wikipedia: Bright Side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Bright_Side), [RationalWiki](https://rationalwiki.org/wiki/Bright_Side)).
- **Content farm-model.** Over 11.000 videoer produceret med et team i ryggen. Det kan en ny kanal ikke kopiere uden at blive ramt af YouTubes regler om "inauthentic content".
- **Din mulighed:** Brug samme format (du-scenarie, animation og nysgerrighed), men med **tjekkede fakta, kilder i beskrivelsen og en fast figur**. Det er netop den "genuine insight", YouTube kræver siden juli 2026, og det skaber tillid hos et voksent publikum med højere RPM.

## 5. Sådan laver du formatet med Higgsfield

- **Faceless Studio:** Vælg kategorien *Education* og stilen *Colorful 3D* (tættest på BrightSides tegneserielook) eller *Pastel Flat 2D*. Stemmen skal være varm og energisk amerikansk engelsk på ca. 150 ord i minuttet.
- **Fast hovedperson:** Lav et character sheet til figuren "You", fx en venlig opdagelsesrejsende med gul hjelm og pandelampe. Den samme figur i alle videoer bliver dit brand.
- **Længde:** 8–10 minutter. Faceless Studio laver episoder på højst 5 minutter, så byg videoen af 2–3 kapitler, eller brug MCP-vejen (op til 10 minutter).
- **Tempo:** Nyt billede hvert 5.–8. sekund. Det giver ca. 70–90 shots til manuskriptet nedenfor.
- **Undertekster:** Brænd **ikke** engelske undertekster ind i billedet, hvis du vil tilføje lydspor på andre sprog. Så står der engelsk tekst over den spanske lyd. Brug YouTubes almindelige undertekster i stedet.
- **Thumbnails:** Lav 3 varianter til YouTubes "Test & Compare".
- **AI-mærkning:** Hold alle scener i samme tegnede stil. Bruger du fotorealistiske scener af virkelige steder eller begivenheder, skal kontakten "Altered or synthetic content" slås til.

## 6. Manuskript: "What If You Dug a Hole Through the Earth?"

**Format:** ca. 8–8½ minut (ca. 1.210 ord speak på 150 ord i minuttet, plus pauser). Videoen er over 8 minutter, så du kan indsætte mid-roll-annoncer. Manuskriptet er på engelsk til det engelske hovedmarked. **Hovedperson:** "You", den tegnede opdagelsesrejsende. **Fast grafik på skærmen:** en dybdemåler (øverst til højre) og et termometer (øverst til venstre), der opdateres ved hvert stop. Det er videoens indbyggede statuslinje.

**Titler til A/B-test:**
1. What If You Dug a Hole Through the Earth?
2. What If You Dug Straight Down and Never Stopped?
3. Scientists Dug 12 KM Into the Earth. Then They Had to Stop.

**Thumbnails (3 varianter):**
- **A:** "You" nederst i et dybt, smalt hul, der ser chokeret op, med orange glød nedefra og en rød pil nedad. Tekst: **"12 KM DOWN"**.
- **B:** Et tværsnit af Jorden med en lille figur i en tunnel og en rød cirkel om den glødende kerne. Tekst: **"HOTTER THAN THE SUN?"**
- **C:** Delt billede: til venstre et barn med en skovl og et overstreget Kina-flag, til højre åbent hav. Tekst: **"NOT CHINA"**.

---

### [0:00–0:30] HOOK
**VISUAL:** Close-up of a rusty, bolted metal cap in snowy ground, surrounded by abandoned concrete ruins. Slow push-in. Cut to an Earth cross-section with a hairline going down from the surface.
**ON-SCREEN:** "12 KM" → "WHY DID THEY STOP?"

> **VO:** In the far north of Russia, near the border with Norway, there's a rusty metal cap bolted to the ground. Underneath it is the deepest hole humans have ever dug: more than twelve kilometers straight down. Scientists drilled for about twenty years… and then they had to give up.
> Today, you're going to pick up where they left off. You're going to dig a hole all the way through the Earth. And by the end, you'll know exactly why nobody has ever done it.

### [0:30–1:10] WHERE WILL YOU COME OUT?
**VISUAL:** You in a backyard with a shovel. A thought bubble shows a Chinese landmark, then pops. A globe spins, and a glowing line shoots through it from the US to the empty southern Indian Ocean, where two tiny islands blink.
**ON-SCREEN:** "~~CHINA~~" → "INDIAN OCEAN" · "Only ~15% of land has land on the other side"

> **VO:** First, let's clear something up. As a kid, you probably heard that if you dig straight down, you'll end up in China. Sorry, for almost everyone, that's not true.
> Start digging anywhere in the lower 48 United States, and you'll come out in the southern Indian Ocean. The only land on the other side is a pair of tiny French islands: St. Paul and Amsterdam. Start in China, and you'd pop out in Argentina or Chile.
> In fact, only about fifteen percent of all land has other land directly on the opposite side of the planet. So whatever happens down there… pack a swimsuit.

### [1:10–2:05] 0 → 4 KM: THE DEEPEST PLACE HUMANS WORK
**HUD:** 0 → 4 km · 15 °C → 66 °C
**VISUAL:** You dig through soil, roots and a lost coin, then clang against gray bedrock. The shovel bends. Transformation shot: you now ride a chunky, cartoon "super-drill". Cutaway to a gold-mine tunnel with miners, pipes of ice slurry, and steam.

> **VO:** The first few meters are the easy part: soil, roots, maybe a lost coin. Then you hit solid rock, and your shovel is useless. So let's upgrade. From here on, you're riding an indestructible, heat-proof super-drill.
> The first thing you notice? It's getting hot. On average, the rock heats up by about twenty-five degrees Celsius for every kilometer you go down.
> At four kilometers, you pass the deepest mine on Earth: the Mponeng gold mine in South Africa. The rock down here reaches sixty-six degrees Celsius, far too hot to touch for long. The mine only works because it pumps thousands of tons of ice slurry underground every day to keep the tunnels below thirty degrees. That's as deep as humans actually go to work. And you've barely started.

### [2:05–4:05] 12 KM: THE KOLA SUPERDEEP BOREHOLE
**HUD:** 12.26 km · 180 °C
**VISUAL:** A 1970s Soviet drilling tower in snowy tundra, with scientists in fur hats. A cross-section compares Mount Everest and the Mariana Trench with the borehole. You peek down a hole narrower than a dinner plate. Glowing microfossils, water droplets squeezing out of rock, and rock bending like warm clay around a drill bit. For the "screams" beat: an old cassette recorder, then a big red "HOAX" stamp.

> **VO:** Twelve kilometers down, you reach the place from the start of this video: the Kola Superdeep Borehole.
> Soviet scientists started drilling here in 1970. By 1989, they'd reached twelve thousand, two hundred and sixty-two meters. That's deeper than Mount Everest is tall, and deeper than the deepest point of the ocean.
> But the hole is only twenty-three centimeters wide. You couldn't fit through it. Only the drill could.
> And what they found down there surprised everyone.
> About six kilometers down, they found fossils of microscopic plankton: tiny sea creatures, turned to stone, buried under miles of rock.
> They found water far deeper than anyone thought possible, squeezed out of the rock itself.
> And they expected the rock to change from granite to basalt at around seven kilometers. It never did.
> Then came the real problem: heat. Scientists expected about one hundred degrees Celsius at the bottom. Instead, they measured one hundred and eighty. At that temperature, the rock started behaving less like stone and more like hot plastic. In the early 1990s, drilling stopped for good.
> Maybe you've heard a creepier version of this story: that scientists lowered a microphone and recorded screams from hell. That's a hoax. The famous "screams" were looped sound effects, reportedly lifted from a 1972 horror movie.
> Here's the part that really hurts: after all that work, the deepest hole in history made it only about a third of the way through the crust beneath it… and just zero point two percent of the way to the center of the Earth.

### [4:05–4:25] QUIZ BREAK
**VISUAL:** You stops, wipes sweat and turns to the camera. A big question card appears with two icons: Earth's core vs. the Sun.
**ON-SCREEN:** "WHAT'S HOTTER? 🌍 vs ☀️ — COMMENT YOUR GUESS"

> **VO:** Quick question before we go any deeper. What do you think is hotter: the center of the Earth… or the surface of the Sun? Drop your guess in the comments right now. The answer is waiting at the bottom.

### [4:25–5:00] 5–70 KM: GOODBYE, CRUST
**HUD:** 40 km · ~1,000 °C
**VISUAL:** An apple sliced in half next to Earth sliced in half, with the thin peel highlighted. Thin crust under the ocean vs. thick crust under mountains. A glowing boundary line ("MOHO") that the drill punches through.

> **VO:** The crust, the thin rocky skin we all live on, is between five and seventy kilometers thick. It's thinnest under the oceans and thickest under mountain ranges. If Earth were an apple, the crust would be thinner than its peel.
> At the bottom, you punch through a boundary geologists call the Moho and enter the mantle. The rock here is around one thousand degrees Celsius.

### [5:00–6:05] THE MANTLE: 2,900 KM OF "SOLID" ROCK
**HUD:** 150 km → 2,900 km · 1,000 °C → 3,700 °C
**VISUAL:** Glowing orange rock slowly flowing like thick taffy, with convection arrows and continents drifting on top. A fingernail next to a ruler for the speed comparison. Sparkling diamonds forming as the drill zooms past. The drill glows red, and You sweats inside a silver heat suit.

> **VO:** Now, you might picture the mantle as an ocean of lava. It isn't. It's almost entirely solid rock. But it's so hot and so squeezed that it slowly flows, about as fast as your fingernails grow. Over millions of years, those slow currents help push the continents around on the surface.
> About a hundred and fifty kilometers down, the heat and pressure are extreme enough to turn carbon into diamonds. Sadly, you can't stop to collect them. You've got nearly three thousand kilometers of mantle to get through.
> And it only gets worse. By the bottom of the mantle, it's about three thousand seven hundred degrees Celsius, and the pressure is more than a million times what you feel at the surface. Your super-drill is glowing. You're starting to regret this.

### [6:05–6:40] THE LIQUID METAL OCEAN (OUTER CORE)
**HUD:** 2,900 → 5,150 km · 4,000+ °C
**VISUAL:** The drill breaks into a swirling ocean of glowing liquid metal. Zoom out to space: magnetic field lines loop around the planet and deflect a stream of solar wind, and an aurora ripples. A compass needle snaps north.

> **VO:** Two thousand nine hundred kilometers down, the rock suddenly ends. You break into an ocean, not of water, but of liquid iron and nickel, well over four thousand degrees Celsius.
> This swirling metal ocean does something amazing. As it churns, it generates Earth's magnetic field: an invisible shield that reaches far out into space and helps protect the planet from the solar wind. It's also why your compass points north. So, please, try not to break it.

### [6:40–7:35] THE INNER CORE: THE ANSWER
**HUD:** 6,371 km · 5,200 °C
**VISUAL:** A giant glowing metal ball, shown side by side with the Moon for size and then with the Sun for heat. A giant hydraulic press squeezes glowing metal. At the exact center, You floats weightless, hat drifting, doing a slow somersault.
**ON-SCREEN:** "5,200 °C ≈ SURFACE OF THE SUN"

> **VO:** Five thousand one hundred and fifty kilometers down, you hit something solid again: the inner core, a ball of iron and nickel about seventy percent the size of the Moon.
> Remember the quiz? Here's the answer. The inner core is about five thousand two hundred degrees Celsius, roughly as hot as the surface of the Sun. So if you guessed "it's a tie," you win.
> But how can metal that hot be solid? Pressure. At the very center, it's around three and a half million times the pressure at the surface. The metal is squeezed so tightly it can't melt.
> And at the exact center of the Earth, something strange happens: you float. The whole planet is pulling on you equally from every direction, so you're weightless.

### [7:35–8:20] THE 38-MINUTE FALL
**VISUAL:** A clean, straight tunnel through the planet, pole to pole. You jumps in. Speed lines and a timer run from 00:00 to 38:00. A pizza delivery scooter appears for the comparison. You pops out on the other side, waves, then falls back in like a pendulum. Last shot: a spinning Earth with a tunnel from somewhere else, and You bonks into the wall.

> **VO:** Now imagine your tunnel is finished: straight through the planet, pole to pole, with all the air pumped out. You jump in.
> You'd fall faster and faster, hitting more than twenty-eight thousand kilometers per hour as you whoosh through the center. Then you'd slow down as you rise toward the other side, and pop out about thirty-eight minutes after you jumped. About as long as a pizza delivery.
> But if nobody catches you, you'll fall right back down… and swing back and forth through the Earth forever.
> Why pole to pole? Because anywhere else, Earth's spin would slam you into the tunnel wall.

### [8:20–8:50] PAYOFF + CTA
**VISUAL:** Pull back from Earth into space, past a tiny Voyager probe. Snap back to the rusty metal cap in Russia. Final shot: You in the backyard next to a tiny hole, shrugging at the camera. End screen.
**ON-SCREEN:** "WHERE WOULD YOUR HOLE COME OUT? 👇"

> **VO:** So why has nobody done it? Because the deepest hole in history covers just zero point two percent of the trip. We've sent a spacecraft more than twenty-four billion kilometers into space, but we've never made it through the thin rocky skin under our own feet.
> Now I'm curious: if you dug straight down from where you're sitting right now, where would you come out? Look up your antipode and tell me in the comments.
> And if you want to know what happens when you go the other way, straight up, watch this video next.
